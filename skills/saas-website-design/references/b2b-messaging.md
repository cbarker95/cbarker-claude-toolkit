# B2B Messaging

B2B-specific buyer psychology, messaging frameworks, and enterprise positioning for SaaS websites. B2B buying is fundamentally different from B2C — multiple stakeholders, longer cycles, higher stakes, and risk aversion. Your messaging must account for all of this.

---

## B2B Buyer Journey Stages

Visitors arrive at different awareness levels. Your page copy should meet them where they are.

### Stage Map
| Stage | What They're Thinking | What They Need |
|-------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Problem-Aware** | "This process is painful but I don't know there's a solution" | Articulate the pain better than they can. Name the problem. |
| **Solution-Aware** | "I know solutions exist, I'm evaluating categories" | Explain your approach/category and why it's the right one. |
| **Product-Aware** | "I know your product, I'm comparing it to alternatives" | Differentiate. Show specific proof. Address objections. |
| **Most-Aware** | "I'm ready to buy, I need the right plan" | Remove friction. Clear pricing. Fast path to conversion. |

### Messaging by Stage

**Homepage** → Handles all stages (routes them to the right content)
**Landing page** → Targets one specific stage (usually problem-aware or solution-aware)
**Pricing page** → Product-aware and most-aware visitors
**Feature page** → Solution-aware and product-aware visitors
**Case study** → Product-aware visitors who need proof to overcome final objections

## Stakeholder Mapping

In B2B, the person using the product is rarely the only decision-maker. Your page may need to speak to multiple audiences.

### Common B2B Stakeholder Roles

| Role | What They Care About | Messaging Approach |
|------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **End User** | Daily workflow improvement, ease of use | "Makes your day easier" — show the product in action |
| **Team Lead** | Team productivity, adoption difficulty | "Your team ships faster" — show aggregate outcomes |
| **Buyer / Manager** | ROI, total cost, vendor reliability | "Reduces cost by X%" — show business metrics |
| **IT / Security** | Compliance, integration, data control | "SOC 2, SSO, self-hosted option" — show trust badges |
| **Executive** | Strategic impact, competitive advantage | "Industry leaders choose Acme" — show authority + scale |

### Practical Application
- **Homepage hero**: Speak to the end user (they arrive first) but include credibility signals for the buyer
- **Feature pages**: End user language + a "For IT" or "Security" subsection
- **Pricing page**: Buyer language + enterprise tier for IT/procurement
- **Case studies**: Manager/executive language with user quotes for credibility

## Enterprise Objection Handling

Enterprise buyers have predictable objections. Address them proactively — don't wait for "Contact Sales."

### The Big 6 Enterprise Objections

| Objection | Page Element | Copy Pattern |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Security** | Trust badge section, footer | "SOC 2 Type II certified. End-to-end encryption. Your data never leaves your VPC." |
| **Compliance** | Dedicated section or page | "GDPR, HIPAA, and SOC 2 compliant. Audit logs for every action." |
| **Integration** | Feature section or integrations page | "Works with your stack: Slack, Jira, GitHub, and 200+ integrations." |
| **Migration** | FAQ or how-it-works section | "Import from [Competitor] in one click. We'll migrate your data for free." |
| **Support** | Pricing tier comparison, footer | "Dedicated CSM for Enterprise. Average response time: 4 minutes." |
| **Scale** | Metrics section, architecture page | "Handles 50M API calls/day. 99.99% uptime SLA." |

### Where to Place Enterprise Signals
- **Above the fold**: One trust signal ("SOC 2 certified" badge or "Enterprise-ready")
- **Feature sections**: Integration logos, security callouts
- **Pricing page**: Enterprise tier with SSO, SAML, audit logs, SLA
- **Footer**: Compliance badges (SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA)
- **Dedicated page**: /security or /enterprise for deep-dive content

## StoryBrand Framework for B2B

Donald Miller's StoryBrand applied to SaaS positioning. The key insight: **your customer is the hero, your product is the guide.**

### The 7-Part Story Structure

```
1. HERO        — Your customer (not your product)
2. PROBLEM     — The villain they're fighting (inefficiency, complexity, risk)
3. GUIDE       — Your product (has empathy + authority)
4. PLAN        — Simple steps to success (3-step how-it-works)
5. CALL        — Clear CTA (what to do next)
6. SUCCESS     — What life looks like with your product
7. FAILURE     — What happens if they do nothing (the cost of inaction)
```

### B2B Application

```
HERO:     "Engineering teams building modern software"
PROBLEM:  "Deployments are slow, risky, and manual"
GUIDE:    "Acme — trusted by 2,800+ engineering teams"
PLAN:     "1. Connect your repo. 2. Configure your pipeline. 3. Deploy."
CALL:     "Start free — deploy in 4 minutes"
SUCCESS:  "Ship every day with zero downtime"
FAILURE:  "Or keep losing weeks to manual deploys and broken releases"
```

### Applying to Page Sections
- **Hero**: Hero (customer) meets Guide (your product)
- **Problem section**: Name the villain explicitly
- **How It Works**: The 3-step plan
- **Testimonials**: Other heroes who succeeded with your guide
- **CTA**: The call to action
- **Final section**: Success vs. failure contrast

## Jobs-to-be-Done for Messaging

Frame your product around the job the customer is hiring it to do — not the features it has.

### Job Categories

| Job Type | Question to Ask | Messaging Implication |
|----------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Functional** | "What task are they trying to complete?" | Feature messaging: "Deploy with one command" |
| **Emotional** | "How do they want to feel?" | Confidence messaging: "Deploy without fear" |
| **Social** | "How do they want to be perceived?" | Status messaging: "The tool top teams choose" |

### B2B Job Statement Template
```
When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome].

"When my team is ready to ship, I want to deploy without manual steps,
 so I can release confidently and move on to the next feature."
```

Use the job statement to write your headline — the outcome is your headline, the situation is your subhead.

## Competitor Differentiation Positioning

### Positioning Against Known Competitors
Don't name competitors directly on your marketing page. Instead, position against the CATEGORY or the OLD WAY.

```
✗ "Better than Competitor X"              — Petty, gives them attention
✓ "The modern alternative to [category]"  — Positions you as the future
✓ "Built for teams who've outgrown [old way]" — Positions them as outdated
```

### Comparison Page Strategy (Separate Page)
For direct comparison pages (/vs/competitor):
- Lead with the job-to-be-done, not the comparison
- Use a factual feature matrix (no spin)
- Include a testimonial from someone who switched
- Address the switching cost directly ("Import in one click")

## Pricing Psychology for B2B

### Value Anchoring
Present the most expensive plan first (or at least make it visible) so the mid-tier feels reasonable.

```
Enterprise: $199/seat   Pro: $49/seat   Starter: $0
                        ↑ Most Popular
```

### Tier Naming
Names should communicate value, not just size:

| Generic | Better | Why |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Basic / Standard / Premium | Starter / Team / Enterprise | Signals who it's for |
| Free / Pro / Business | Build / Scale / Transform | Signals what it enables |
| Small / Medium / Large | Individual / Team / Organization | Signals the buyer |

### Enterprise CTA
Enterprise buyers expect a conversation. Don't make "Contact Sales" the only option — pair it with a self-serve trial:

```
Enterprise:
  "Talk to sales"           — For procurement-driven buyers
  "Start a free trial"      — For technical evaluators
  "See enterprise features" — For researchers
```

### Price Display
- Show monthly price even for annual plans: "$49/seat/month, billed annually"
- Show savings: "Save 20% with annual billing"
- Per-seat pricing communicates scale; flat pricing communicates simplicity
- If your price is high, justify with ROI: "$99/month saves 10+ hours/week"
