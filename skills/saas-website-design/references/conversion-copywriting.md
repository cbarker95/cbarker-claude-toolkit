# Conversion Copywriting

Actionable frameworks for writing B2B SaaS copy that converts. Every framework here is a tool — use the one that fits the context, not all of them at once.

---

## Headline Formulas

Headlines do 80% of the work. A great headline makes visitors stay; a weak one makes them bounce. Every headline should pass two tests: (1) Is it specific? (2) Does it communicate an outcome?

### Formula 1: Outcome + Specificity
```
[Desired outcome] + [specific mechanism or proof]

"Deploy in 4 minutes, not 4 sprints"
"Ship 2x faster with zero coordination overhead"
"One dashboard for every metric that matters"
```

### Formula 2: [Do X] without [Pain Y]
```
[Achieve desirable thing] without [the thing they dread]

"Scale your infrastructure without hiring DevOps"
"Get enterprise security without enterprise complexity"
"Collaborate in real-time without another Slack thread"
```

### Formula 3: From [Bad State] to [Good State]
```
"From 3-day deploys to 3-minute deploys"
"From spreadsheet chaos to a single source of truth"
"From 'who owns this?' to automatic accountability"
```

### Formula 4: Specific Number + Outcome
```
"10,000+ teams ship faster with Acme"
"Cut your deploy time by 73%"
"3 steps from idea to production"
```

### Formula 5: Question That Implies the Answer
```
"What if your whole team could ship without waiting?"
"Still coordinating releases over Slack?"
"Why do the best engineering teams choose Acme?"
```

**Avoid these headline patterns:**
- Starting with "Welcome to..." or "Introducing..."
- Listing 3+ features separated by commas
- Using "powerful", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge"
- Asking questions the visitor doesn't care about

## The "So What?" Test

After writing any claim, ask "So what?" until you reach something the reader cares about. If you can't get there in 2-3 levels, the original claim isn't worth making.

```
Claim: "Real-time collaboration"
So what? → "Your team works in the same space"
So what? → "No more waiting for handoffs or status updates"
So what? → "You ship features in days instead of weeks"

Final copy: "Ship features in days — your whole team works in one space, no handoffs needed."
```

Apply this test to every headline, subhead, bullet point, and CTA. The bottom of the "So What?" chain is always the copy you should use.

## PAS Framework (Problem → Agitate → Solve)

The most reliable structure for landing page copy. Works because it mirrors how buyers think: they feel a pain, realize it's worse than they thought, then want a solution.

### Structure
1. **Problem** — Name the specific pain your audience feels (use their words, not yours)
2. **Agitate** — Make the pain vivid. What does it cost them? What happens if it continues?
3. **Solve** — Present your product as the resolution, with proof

### B2B Example
```
PROBLEM:
Your team spends more time coordinating than building.

AGITATE:
Every feature takes 3 meetings, 2 Slack threads, and a spreadsheet update
before anyone writes a line of code. Meanwhile, your competitors are shipping
weekly. The coordination tax isn't just frustrating — it's costing you market share.

SOLVE:
Acme gives your team one workspace where planning, building, and shipping
happen together. Teams using Acme deploy 3x more often with 60% fewer meetings.
Start free →
```

## Before / After / Bridge

Show the contrast between life without and with your product. Effective for feature sections, testimonial framing, and homepage scrolls.

### Structure
1. **Before** — Their world today (pain, frustration, inefficiency)
2. **After** — Their world with your product (ease, speed, clarity)
3. **Bridge** — Your product is the bridge between the two

### Application Pattern
```
BEFORE: Scattered feedback in emails, Slack, and docs. Nothing actionable.
AFTER:  Every insight tagged, prioritized, and linked to your roadmap.
BRIDGE: Acme turns raw customer feedback into roadmap-ready opportunities — automatically.
```

## Feature → Advantage → Benefit (FAB) Translation

Most SaaS copy stops at features. Great copy translates features into outcomes the buyer cares about.

| Feature (What it does) | Advantage (Why it matters) | Benefit (What they get) |
|------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Real-time sync | Everyone sees the latest version | No more "which doc is current?" |
| Role-based permissions | Right access for right people | Ship with confidence, no accidental changes |
| One-click deploy | No manual config or DevOps | Go from code to production in minutes |
| Audit logging | Full visibility into changes | Pass compliance audits without scrambling |
| API-first architecture | Integrates with existing tools | Works with your stack, not against it |

**Always write the benefit column.** The feature column is for the docs. The benefit column is for the marketing page.

## Specificity Techniques

Vague copy fails. Specific copy converts. Replace every generic claim with a concrete one.

### Vague → Specific Substitutions
| Vague | Specific |
|-------|---------|
| "Save time" | "Cut onboarding from 2 weeks to 2 hours" |
| "Easy to use" | "Set up in 4 minutes with zero configuration" |
| "Trusted by many" | "Trusted by 2,847 teams including Stripe and Ramp" |
| "Fast performance" | "P99 latency under 50ms" |
| "Great support" | "Average response time: 4 minutes during business hours" |
| "Affordable" | "Starts at $0/month. Scales to $49/seat when you're ready" |
| "Secure" | "SOC 2 Type II certified. Data encrypted at rest and in transit" |

### The Rule
If a claim could apply to any product in your category, it's not specific enough. Rewrite it until it could only describe YOUR product.

## Power Words vs. Weak Words

### Use These (Concrete, Active, Specific)
ship, deploy, build, cut, eliminate, automate, replace, track, measure, own, control, launch, scale, reduce, accelerate, simplify

### Avoid These (Vague, Passive, Overused)
leverage, utilize, empower, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, powerful, innovative, next-generation, synergy, holistic, end-to-end, state-of-the-art

### The Test
Read the sentence aloud. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it. If it sounds like something a smart coworker would say, keep it.
